# Creative rules

## Style selection

Choose the strongest style for the topic rather than forcing UGC. Options can include UGC, cinematic, POV, documentary, interview, product demo, testimonial, family drama, emotional storytelling, stop motion, explainer motion graphics, comedy skit, mini people, giant product, news report, animation, anime, fantasy, alien, zombie, character marketing, house tour, luxury commercial, vlog, social experiment, trend remix, and aesthetic B-roll. Combine styles when the combination improves the concept.

Useful pairings include medical cards with emotional cinematic POV, property with mini people plus house tour, coffee with stop motion plus aesthetic B-roll, hibah with family drama plus emotional storytelling, and cancer awareness with documentary plus emotional POV. These are examples, not fixed mappings.

## Story structure

Select the most fitting narrative structure internally: problem-agitation-solution, attention-interest-desire-action, narrative storytelling, hero journey, problem versus solution, before and after, curiosity loop, emotional journey, documentary flow, comedy setup and punchline, mystery reveal, or transformation. Never name the framework in the user-facing result. Do not force the same hook-problem-solution-CTA structure onto every concept.

## Duration

| Duration | Scenes |
|---:|---:|
| 10 seconds | 4 |
| 20 seconds | 8 |
| 30 seconds | 12 |
| 60 seconds | 24 |

For another duration, calculate approximately one scene per 2.5 seconds and use sensible scene boundaries.

## Scene quality

Every scene must connect to the previous scene, move the narrative forward, show progression and visual movement, create curiosity, and build momentum. Avoid random, repetitive, or disconnected scenes.

Use dynamic, cinematic visuals and varied framing. Select camera language deliberately from extreme close-up, close-up, medium shot, wide shot, drone shot, POV, over-the-shoulder, tracking, dolly in, dolly out, push in, pull back, handheld, and static.

## Special styles

Mini People uses tiny characters interacting with a giant environment or product. Giant Product makes the product dominate the frame while people interact around it. POV is seen through the character's eyes. Stop motion uses many micro-actions, fast transitions, and minimal dialogue. Explainer motion graphics prioritizes clarity and visual hierarchy. Documentary feels realistic and authentic. Cinematic prioritizes emotion, visual storytelling, and film-like composition.
